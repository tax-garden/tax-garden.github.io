# Capital Gains Tax India AY 2026–27: LTCG, STCG, Property & Shares Explained

The Finance Act 2024 introduced some of the biggest changes to capital gains tax in India in more than a decade. For Assessment Year 2026–27 (income earned during FY 2025–26), these updated rules apply fully for the first time.

If you sold:

- Shares  
- Mutual Funds  
- Property  
- Land  
- Gold  
- Bonds  
- Crypto Assets  

…understanding these new rules before filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) is extremely important.

This guide explains the latest capital gains tax rules in simple language.

---

## What is Capital Gains Tax?

Capital gains tax is the tax paid on the profit earned from selling a capital asset.

A capital asset can include:

- Equity Shares  
- Mutual Funds  
- Residential Property  
- Commercial Property  
- Gold  
- Bonds  
- Crypto Assets  
- Unlisted Shares  

If the selling price is higher than the purchase price, the profit is called a capital gain.

The tax treatment depends on:

- Asset type  
- Holding period  
- Applicable exemptions  

---

# Quick Summary of Capital Gains Tax Rules for AY 2026–27

## Equity Assets

Applicable to:

- Listed Shares  
- Equity Mutual Funds (with STT)

### LTCG

- Gains above Rs 1.25 lakh
- Taxed at 12.5%
- Covered under Section 112A

### STCG

- Taxed at 20%
- Covered under Section 111A

---

## Non-Equity Assets

Includes:

- Land
- Building
- Gold
- Unlisted Shares
- Bonds

### LTCG

- Flat 12.5%
- No indexation

### STCG

- Added to slab income
- Taxed according to slab rates

---

## Crypto and Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs)

### Tax Rules

- Flat 30% tax
- Covered under Section 115BBH

### Important Restriction

- No set-off of losses against other income

---

# Equity Capital Gains: New Rates You Must Know

Before July 23, 2024:

- LTCG = 10%
- STCG = 15%

After July 23, 2024:

- LTCG = 12.5%
- STCG = 20%

These new rates apply to most transactions in FY 2025–26.

---

## Holding Period for Equity

### More than 12 months

→ Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG)

### Less than 12 months

→ Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG)

---

## Example: Equity LTCG Calculation

Suppose you sold listed shares in December 2025.

- Profit = Rs 5 lakh
- Holding period = 18 months

Since the shares were held for more than 12 months, the gain becomes LTCG.

### Calculation

LTCG = Rs 5 lakh – Rs 1.25 lakh exemption  
= Rs 3.75 lakh

Tax = 12.5% × Rs 3.75 lakh  
= Rs 46,875

Plus surcharge and cess if applicable.

---

# Non-Equity Assets: New 24-Month Rule

For:

- Property
- Land
- Gold
- Bonds
- Unlisted Shares

…the long-term holding period is now:

## 24 Months

Earlier, immovable property required 36 months.

This change was introduced under the Finance Act 2024.

---

# Debt Mutual Funds Special Rule

Debt mutual funds acquired on or after April 1, 2023:

- Do not get LTCG treatment
- Do not get indexation benefit

All gains are treated as:

## Short-Term Capital Gains

Taxed according to slab rates under Section 50AA.

# Property Sale and Indexation Decision

One of the most important changes in AY 2026–27 relates to property taxation.

Your purchase date determines which tax option you can choose.

---

## Property Purchased On or After July 23, 2024

Only one option available:

### 12.5% Tax Without Indexation

---

## Property Purchased On or Before July 22, 2024

You can choose whichever gives lower tax:

### Option A

12.5% without indexation

### Option B

20% with indexation

---

# Worked Example: Property Tax Comparison

Property purchased:

- April 2015
- Purchase Price = Rs 40 lakh

Sold in:

- January 2026
- Sale Price = Rs 1.10 crore

---

## Option A: Without Indexation

Capital Gain:

Rs 70 lakh

Tax:

12.5%

### Tax Liability

Rs 8,75,000

---

## Option B: With Indexation

Indexed Cost:

Rs 40 lakh × 363 / 254  
≈ Rs 57.2 lakh

Capital Gain:

Rs 1.10 crore – Rs 57.2 lakh  
= Rs 52.8 lakh

Tax:

20%

### Tax Liability

Rs 10,56,000

---

## Result

Option A is cheaper by approximately Rs 1.8 lakh.

However, for older properties, indexation may still provide better savings.

Always calculate both options before filing your return.

# Important Capital Gains Exemptions

## Section 54

Applicable when:

- Selling a residential property
- Reinvesting LTCG into another residential house

### Time Limits

- 1 year before sale
- 2 years after sale
- 3 years for construction

### Maximum Exemption

Rs 10 crore

---

## Section 54F

Applicable when:

- Entire net sale consideration is reinvested

Conditions:

- Available to Individuals and HUFs
- Must not own more than one residential house on sale date

### Maximum Exemption

Rs 10 crore

---

## Section 54EC

Applicable when LTCG from land or building is invested into:

- NHAI Bonds
- REC Bonds
- PFC Bonds
- IRFC Bonds

### Investment Limit

Rs 50 lakh

### Lock-In Period

5 years

Available for all taxpayers including companies.

# Which ITR Form Should You Use?

Capital gains are reported under:

## Schedule CG

### ITR-2

For:

- Individuals with capital gains
- No business income

### ITR-3

For:

- Individuals with business income and capital gains

### ITR-1 (Sahaj)

Cannot be used if you sold capital assets.
